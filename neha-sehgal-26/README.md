## *Psychosocial stress modifies the association between polybrominated diphenyl ethers (PBDEs) and maternal and placental inflammation during mid-gestation*

[DOI]

PMID: 

### Abstract

Prior research has implicated maternal and fetal inflammation because of exposure to polybrominated diphenyl ethers (PBDEs) and psychosocial stressors. However, no studies have examined their joint effects, despite knowledge that exposure to PBDEs and stressors co-occur.  
Participants were pregnant women undergoing elective second-trimester terminations in the San Francisco Bay Area (N=63). We measured 20 cytokines and 4 PBDEs in maternal serum and placental tissue obtained during the second trimester. Participants self-reported psychosocial stressors, including job strain, perceived stress, stressful life events, and social support. Stressors were summarized using latent class analysis (LCA), which identified two distinct subgroups reflecting high stress (N=33) and low stress (N=30). We examined whether associations between PBDEs and inflammation were modified by levels of the two stress clusters using linear regression and quantile g-computation. 
In general, we observed significant stress-modified associations in the placenta. Placental levels of BDE-47, -99, -100, and -153 were positively associated with IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, CCL-3, CCL-4, CXCL-10, and TNF-RI in the low-stress cluster, while these associations were null or inverse in the high-stress cluster. This pattern persisted for the BDE mixtures, which also indicated stronger positive joint effects of BDEs on placental inflammatory markers among low-stress participants. However, the pattern of the stress-modified associations in serum trended differently and was more attenuated than that observed in placenta.  
We find that the association between placental PBDE concentrations and inflammatory markers is modified by psychosocial stress, with effects greater in magnitude among those participants experiencing lower stress, which was inconsistent with our hypothesis. Our findings suggested that combined PBDEs and stress could affect immune function with adverse perinatal consequences.
